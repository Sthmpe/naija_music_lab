# Get Layd combined-workflow evaluation — 2026-09-27

This evaluates the first project with local PCM intake, working lyric-source comparison, separate audio-capable Listening Analyst reports and Codex evidence integration. The user has since approved a documented source-limited first-pass gate; the earlier recommendations below are read in that light.

1. **Batching preparation:** useful for one technical intake, source checks and standardized handoffs. Independence was preserved because each song has its own report, lyric log, analysis and status. It would fail if project similarities were copied into song findings before song verification; this batch avoided that.
2. **One broad listening pass:** sufficient for a first song-level pass in Lo Lo and Bad Influence alongside pre-existing lyric research; Damn needed a targeted second pass because its initial report lacked exact lyric alignment and gave conflicting tempo/section estimates. The You and Ye Ye Ye reports sufficiently cover their available audio. Their endings remain unavailable, but no tail checks are required for the user-approved first pass.
3. **Recurring follow-up questions:** exact high-value word versus alternate source word; counting layered hook events; beat placement/pickup when a phrase mechanism depends on it; identity or text of response voices; whether the ending is present. Complete audio intake before listening would eliminate avoidable tail follow-up.
4. **Request length:** song-specific packets were appropriately focused on disputed phrases and measurements, but a compact table with 5–10 priority windows plus an explicit version/ending check would reduce analyst effort. Long general prompts encourage diffuse prose and weaker timestamp certainty.
5. **High-value schema elements:** timestamped lyric/audio interaction, source conflict plus phonetic confidence, pulse provenance, repetition shape, negative space with instrumental continuation, narrative change before refrain and separate generative hypotheses. These explain *why* a line works musically.
6. **Lower-value prose:** repeating “key unmeasured / exact grid unknown” in every subsection and generic claims of emotional mood without an audible or textual mechanism. Keep these in a single limitations block and in structured nulls; do not omit required metrics.
7. **Before What Have We Done:** Get Layd is closed within documented coverage. For new recordings, use reference duration only to flag files for review, then ask the analyst whether the supplied version audibly resolves; ask for 5–10 exact phrase windows, beat interval samples, role of backing answers, and phonetic transcription of disputed local words; have technical analysis output machine-readable values with provenance. A substantial but truncated source can be analysed to its boundary if the source-limited gate is met; do not treat severely corrupted or tiny excerpts as automatically sufficient. Do not promote new metrics to required schema fields until tested against another project.
8. **Scale to 30+ songs:** the division of labour is workable if capture coverage is assessed before handoff, reports remain per-song, JSON updates are schema-validated, and only material disputes get targeted follow-ups. Complete captures remain preferable, but the approved source-limited gate prevents disproportionate delays when substantial representative audio is already analysed. Current manual prose and repeated source recitation will be costly at 30+ songs; adopt reusable evidence tables and concise structured findings after validating on the next project. This is a qualified readiness assessment, not proof of throughput at scale.

**Decision:** the user explicitly approved the source-limited first-pass gate, now documented in `DECISIONS.md` and `docs/methodology.md`. The other workflow ideas remain proposals for later testing.
