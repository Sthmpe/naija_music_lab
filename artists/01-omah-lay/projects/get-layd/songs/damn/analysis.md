## Transferable original-songwriting lessons — single-song evidence

These are **generative principles** abstracted from this recording, not directions to reproduce its lyrics, melody, hook words or identifiable arrangement.

1. **Cadence and phrase length:** Build a family of compact phrases with varied entry timing and rest length. Let unstressed setup syllables compress while a few destination syllables get more time; avoid making every line the same syllable count.
2. **Repetition and hook construction:** Keep a memory unit small enough to recur clearly. Preserve its rhythmic identity, then vary the semantic setup and the backing density so a later return carries new weight without requiring more words.
3. **Phonetics and Pidgin/Nigerian-English delivery:** Test words in the mouth against the beat. Allow natural speech stress, relaxed boundaries and local pronunciation to shape timing; do not force formal spelling or invented dialect markers to do the musical work.
4. **Negative space:** Write intentional gaps after short statements. A continuing rhythm section can sustain momentum and make a phrase feel complete without filling every subdivision with vocals.
5. **Vocal/instrument interaction:** Plan what bass, percussion or a backing texture does *during and just after* a phrase. Re-entry and added support can make a repeated line feel larger; keep the identity of any extra vocal layer uncertain until evidence distinguishes it.
