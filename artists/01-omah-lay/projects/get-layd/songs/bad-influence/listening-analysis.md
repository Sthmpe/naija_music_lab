## Why Simple Lines Work

A strong example is the plain accusation ending in:

**“scatter my plans.”**

**~0:10.5–0:13.0 — [clear]**

Its impact comes from arrangement and cadence rather than sophisticated wording:

1. the preceding words are rhythmically compressed;
2. the line enters close to the stable pulse;
3. **“plans”** receives the melodic/stress landing;
4. the vowel is given more space than the setup;
5. the singer stops;
6. bass/percussion remain active in the resulting vocal gap;
7. the next accusation arrives before the previous landing has completely left the listener's memory.

The sentence therefore works simultaneously as language, rhythm and percussion.

The repeated clause cascade applies the opposite trick: instead of enlarging a simple ending, it eventually **removes the expected ending**, making incompleteness itself memorable.
