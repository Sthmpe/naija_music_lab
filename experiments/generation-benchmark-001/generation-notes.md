# Generation notes — benchmark 001

LAB_STATE_AT_GENERATION: Get Layd first-pass complete; later Omah Lay projects not yet analysed

## Internal concept comparison

Three substantially different approaches were explored privately without retaining full alternate lyrics: **a meal left on the stove**, **a key left in place**, and **a once-frequent call reduced to one brief signal**. The call concept gave the clearest small hook and let the story turn through behavior rather than a declaration of love. The meal detail remains in the opening as context, not a second competing hook.

The final revision made the refrain change meaning: the first return is a defensive promise; the second admits that listening was the problem; the last leaves the promise unanswered. It also replaced a generic late-night confession with the visible phone and dark screen. The complete final version is in `generated-lyrics.md`; weaker full drafts are not presented.

## Cadence and arrangement intentions

- **Pocket:** intended around 104 BPM, without a certified melody or beat grid. The short “One ring, I pick” cell should feel clipped and repeatable; do not stretch `pick` merely to fill a bar.
- **Compressed setups:** “Closed the shop in Yaba,” “I blamed traffic at Ojuelegba,” and “I nearly said, ‘After December’” need conversational pickups and quick low-stress syllables. Keep Yaba and Ojuelegba intelligible rather than squeezing their names to fit a rigid syllable count.
- **Longer landings:** hold `twice`, then `time`, then the open vowel in `calling` a little longer on successive hooks. These are suggested landing words, not a fixed tune.
- **Major rests:** the intro’s two-beat gap, the pause after “thumbs-up,” the gap before each hook’s last cell, and the pause after the ledger image. Let the groove continue under the vocal rests.
- **Instrument answers:** a small plucked or percussive response can occupy the hook gap and answer the truncated final “One ring…”; this is a new arrangement proposal, not a sound measured from the research recordings.
- **Delivery arc:** the first hook is firm and almost too certain; the second loses that edge; the final incomplete cell should sound like an unfinished thought. Avoid a large climactic run that would falsely resolve the ending.

## Benchmark limits

The draft has not been sung or heard at tempo. Naturalness, bar fit, melodic contour and one-listen recall remain hypotheses to test with a Nigerian vocalist and a demo. The handful of Lagos references serve this new plot and are not claims that the Get Layd project establishes a standard city vocabulary.
