# Research trace — benchmark 001

LAB_STATE_AT_GENERATION: Get Layd first-pass complete; later Omah Lay projects not yet analysed

The supporting evidence is the [Get Layd first-pass project synthesis](../../artists/01-omah-lay/projects/get-layd/project-profile.md) and its five independent song analyses. The source has three recordings with audible endings and two substantial truncated captures. These are **project-supported hypotheses**, not universal Nigerian songwriting rules. No source-song lyric or melody was used in the new draft.

## Project-supported mechanisms used

| Research principle → how it affected this original draft | Evidence status |
|---|---|

| Changing phrase density before a familiar refrain can change its emotional reading → the second verse uses a longer conversation and a pause at the ledger before the short hook returns. | Project hypothesis with clearest support in 3/5 available portions; final endings of two source tracks unassessable. `USE_CASE: STORY_EDITOR; CADENCE_DOCTOR` |
| Natural Nigerian speech stress matters more than adding ornamental slang → the lyric uses one ordinary “I dey try” admission and otherwise lets local details and conversation establish place. | Probable project pattern; this generated phrase is an editorial choice requiring fluent-speaker review. `USE_CASE: NIGERIAN_LANGUAGE_CRITIC` |

## Choices invented for this benchmark

The shop in Yaba, Ojuelegba traffic, food message, ledger, three-to-one call change, “one ring” hook, cup and face-up phone are **new story choices**. Their particular combination and the final dark-screen image are not findings from Get Layd. The intended 104 BPM pocket follows the test brief; no claim is made that one project requires that tempo. The proposed plucked/percussive answer is an arrangement experiment, not a verified production prescription.

This trace records influence, not proof that the finished lyric is musically effective. Later reruns should keep the same brief and compare sung naturalness, hook recall, narrative clarity and originality under similar evaluation conditions.
