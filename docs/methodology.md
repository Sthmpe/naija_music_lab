# Methodology

1. Inventory an artist's original albums, EPs, mixtapes, substantial collaborations, expanded editions, singles and notable features chronologically. Use primary release pages where possible.
2. Recommend up to five distinct substantial original projects as a default; fewer if fewer exist. Catalogue deluxe editions as extensions of their original eras. Explain why older work belongs. Obtain user project approval.
3. For approved projects, show every track and a preliminary metadata row. Analyse complete projects when they have approximately five tracks; use representative sampling for larger ones. Surface solo standalone recordings separately from collaborations. Use special comparative cases for remixes/alternate versions and validation tracks when later findings need testing. Obtain user song approval.
4. For a collaboration lab, first verify the artist's actual vocal and creative contribution; credit presence alone is insufficient. Propose an informative sample for user approval, then analyse those cases independently. Later write `collaboration-behaviour.md` using the standard template, without premature pattern claims.
5. Create one analysis pair per approved song. Observe and measure structure, cadence, phonetics, language, story, hook, vocal and production features; mark inaccessible audio measurements unknown.
6. Synthesize within a project only after every approved song has a complete first-pass analysis of the available, substantial source material. Keep **source recording completeness** separate from **analysis completeness for available audio**. A truncated but substantial recording may support a source-limited first-pass song analysis when listening evidence covers the available portion, the cutoff is identified, and post-cutoff conclusions are withheld. A missing ending is unknown evidence, not proof that a technique is absent. Do not apply this exception automatically to very short, badly corrupted or otherwise unrepresentative sources. Label project synthesis with the coverage of each song. Synthesize the artist only when all selected project, standalone and collaboration song analyses meet their documented evidence gates.
7. Build cross-artist findings only from multiple completed artists. Track sample size and contrary examples.

## Experimental musical-language dimensions (added after Generation Benchmark 001)

Keep the existing cadence, phonetics, language, hook, lyric/audio, and technical requirements. For a few consequential phrases per song, record a short cue and approximate timestamp, then examine the following where audible evidence permits. These are qualitative research prompts, not compulsory scores or claims that a written lyric was heard. Distinguish text-derived observations from Listening Analyst reports, measurements, and interpretations. Mark unavailable evidence unknown.

- **Singability / mouth-feel:** Ask why a phrase is physically satisfying to sing. Note open or closed vowel endings, consonant-cluster ease or softening, repeatable mouth shapes, sustain-friendly syllables, and performance pronunciation that differs from writing. Physical ease is an interpretation unless tested by a singer; do not infer it from spelling alone.
- **Phonetic stickiness:** Identify vowel families, repeated syllables, near/internal rhyme, consonant attacks, meaningful alliteration, alternating sounds and nonlexical units. For a hook, optionally name `PHONETIC_NUCLEUS`: the smallest heard sound or word unit plausibly carrying much of its recall. This is an analytical hypothesis, not a measured memory score.
- **Melodic affordance of words:** Connect phonetic shape to observed pickups, held syllables, phrase endings, melodic landings and repeated anchors. Use contour/function descriptions, not copied melodies. If pitch or landing is not heard or measured, record a possible affordance rather than a performed fact.
- **Nigerian conversational rhythm:** Where supported, describe Nigerian English or Pidgin syntax, fragments, omission, contractions, code-switching, interjections, address words, speech stress, informal endings, interruptions, rhetorical questions and economy. Quote only tiny cues. Separate heard pronunciation, proposed spelling, meaning and naturalness confidence; seek fluent-speaker review when needed. Never treat a stereotype or inserted slang as evidence of local naturalness.
- **Phrase looseness:** Mark unequal lengths, incomplete thoughts, interrupted lines, repeated fragments, one-word replies, ad-libs, vocables and deliberate silence; explain how the performance or arrangement completes the phrase. Grammatical incompleteness is not inherently effective.
- **Semantic versus sonic load:** For each selected moment, optionally label `SEMANTIC_DOMINANT`, `SONIC_DOMINANT` or `BALANCED`, then name the actual contributors: meaning, sound, cadence, melody, delivery and production. Labels are qualitative judgments tied to evidence, not objective scores or whole-song verdicts.
- **Hook phonetic engineering:** Beyond counting words and repetitions, document the smallest repeatable unit, vowel/syllable profile, ease of repetition, chant/call-response potential, whether stable sound acquires new meaning, and whether the heard sound has appeal without full context. Distinguish observed repetition from speculative audience memorability.
- **Vocable / nonlexical behaviour:** Treat `oh`, `ah`, `ye/yeah`, `mm`, shortened names/words, repeated syllables and other sounds as possible rhythm, breathing, emotion, transition, hook, response or mouth-feel devices. Record their performed function when audible; do not default to "filler."

### Prospective mechanism checks for the next projects

These checks extend the dimensions above. Use them on a few consequential moments with source coverage, not as a checklist that forces a finding in every song.

- **Bounce (`BOUNCE_MECHANISM`):** Describe the actual short/long and stressed/unstressed alternation, pickup, syncopation, repeating rhythm cell, gap, consonant attack, percussion answer or call/response that creates motion. Record where the phrase lands against the beat when known. "Bouncy" alone is not an explanation.
- **Inside-the-line groove (`INTERNAL_RHYTHM_PATTERN`):** Map syllable groups, micro-pauses, compressed clusters, accent placement, local acceleration/relaxation and internal-rhyme landings. Ask whether a plausible groove survives without the drums. Distinguish a heard pattern from a pattern inferred only from the written line.
- **Rhyme to the ear (`PHONETIC_RHYME_PATTERN`):** Compare performed vowel and consonant sounds, not just visual spelling or end words. Include assonance, consonance, slant/near/internal rhyme and pronunciation-dependent rhyme; note when Nigerian English or Pidgin delivery changes the sound relationship. Do not assign a phonetic rhyme from uncertain transcription alone.
- **Purpose of switching (`CODE_SWITCH_FUNCTION`):** When English and Pidgin or another language alternate, test rhythm, syllable economy, rhyme, intimacy, emphasis, humour, cultural specificity, conversational naturalness and melodic landing as candidate functions. A switch's *observed position* can be clear while the artist's intention remains unknown. Do not treat switching as decoration or assume that one language is inherently more intimate or rhythmic.
- **Playfulness:** Separate `PLAYFUL_IN_MEANING` (teasing, reversal, comparison, exaggeration), `PLAYFUL_IN_SOUND` (fun-to-pronounce syllables or sound twists) and `PLAYFUL_IN_DELIVERY` (reaction, timing or vocal performance). Describe an actual cue and preserve sombre songs as counterexamples to any generalization.
- **Reward from repetition (`REPETITION_REWARD_MECHANISM`):** Label exact, rhythmic, phonetic, semantic, progressive or call/response repetition. Beyond counts, test whether setup, duration, vowel, backing voice, arrangement, gap, anticipation or changed meaning gives a return fresh value. Listener desire or addictiveness is a hypothesis until tested; document the observable variation first.
- **Memorable vocal sound (`VOCAL_SOUND_NUCLEUS`):** For a vocable, hum, cry, ad-lib, shortened name or response, identify the smallest recurring heard sound and its hook, transition, rhythm, emotion, breathing or response role. Keep this distinct from `PHONETIC_NUCLEUS`, which may be lexical.
- **Wording that suggests melody (`MELODIC_AFFORDANCE`; `LANDING_WORD_BEHAVIOUR`):** Check spoken intonation, stress, syllable grouping, vowel sequence, segmentation and repeated endings for a possible rise, fall or sustain. For an actually heard landing, record stress, vowel openness, duration, semantic role and whether the sound persists under changing setups. A phrase that seems melodic on paper is only a hypothesis until performed; do not reconstruct source pitches or copyrighted melody.
- **Mouth-feel detail (`MOUTH_FEEL`; `SINGABILITY`):** Where useful note lip/tongue travel, swallowed consonants, softened clusters, shortened syllables and open-vowel release, in addition to the existing singability checks. Perceived physical ease needs singer or fluent-speaker testing before it is stated as fact.
- **Conversational looseness:** Ask what musical or social function an incomplete, response-like or informally phrased line serves, and what a perfectly grammatical rewrite would lose. Test naturalness with an appropriate speaker; grammatical deviation alone does not establish Nigerian usage.

For future Hook Doctor evidence, retain lexical simplicity, phonetic/vocal-sound nucleus, vowel profile, internal rhythm, chantability, repetition reward, variation, melodic affordance, rests and response potential. For Cadence Doctor evidence, retain bounce, speech stress, grouping, pickups, compression, landing, phrase-length variation and beat relationship. For Nigerian Language Critic evidence, retain attested syntax and pronunciation, code-switch function, slang context, speech economy, playfulness and fluent-speaker uncertainty. These are research inputs for future specialists, not implementations of them.

## Slang and contemporary language intelligence (experimental)

For a **meaningful** slang, street or local expression, coinage, shortened form, cultural reference or unusual conversational construction, create an optional phrase-level language observation. Do not catalogue every everyday word. Keep a short cue and source/timestamp, not a full copyrighted line. This layer extends the existing Nigerian-English, Pidgin, phonetic and code-switch analysis; it does not replace it. Record only what the available text, audio, knowledgeable speaker or contemporary source can establish.

1. **Form and provenance:** Preserve the heard pronunciation, proposed written form, language/register, any shortening or performance alteration, source song and observation date. Keep phonetic confidence separate from orthographic confidence. Mark uncertain spelling rather than normalizing it.
2. **Meaning in context:** Distinguish sourced/verified meaning, plausible interpretation and alternate meanings. Note literal, figurative, teasing, boastful, romantic, insulting or humorous force only when context supports it. Record whether Nigerian context is necessary for interpretation; do not infer a speaker's demographic identity from one expression.
3. **Songwriting function (`SLANG_FUNCTION`):** Explain why the expression is useful *in this phrase*: syllable economy, compression, rhyme, phonetic pleasure, local identity, intimacy, humour, attitude, character voice, emphasis, quotability, response, melodic landing or natural speech. A possible function is an interpretation, not a proven writer intention.
4. **Rhythmic and phonetic value (`SLANG_PHONETIC_VALUE`):** Compare its syllables, stress, clipping, stretch, landing vowel, consonant attack, internal rhythm, rhyme family, chantability and mouth-feel with an **abstract** formal-English alternative. Avoid reproducing or rewriting long protected lines. State whether the performed form, rather than just spelling, creates the advantage.
5. **Switch moment (`CODE_SWITCH_TRIGGER`):** When language/register changes, locate the switch and test rhythm, intimacy, humour, attitude, rhyme, emotional escalation, phrasing economy, cultural specificity, conversational fit and melodic affordance as possible triggers. Distinguish the observed switch from inferred cause. A change of language is not inherently decoration or authenticity.
6. **Social/character role:** Ask what the expression contributes to the narrator's voice or relationship in this song. Age, street awareness, confidence, vulnerability and social setting are interpretations requiring independent support; do not assign demographic identity from slang alone.
7. **Quotability (`QUOTABILITY_MECHANISM`):** Where relevant, examine simplicity, humour, attitude, recognisable everyday phrasing, caption potential, response potential and sound ease. These are candidate mechanisms; audience recall or commercial effect needs separate evidence. Never claim that slang alone made a song a hit.
8. **Freshness:** With a dated source, mark established usage, currently fashionable expression, regional usage, candidate coinage, possibly dated usage or `UNCERTAIN`. Date every time-sensitive assessment. A term on an old recording is evidence of use at that time, not proof of present currency.
9. **Reuse risk:** Track overuse or cliché only after comparison across multiple independent songs/artists and dates. Separately flag terms closely associated with one artist/song and likely to invite imitation. One occurrence does not establish cliché.
10. **Scope:** Classify cautiously as `ARTIST_SPECIFIC_CANDIDATE`, `SCENE_WIDE_CANDIDATE`, `GENERAL_NIGERIAN_USAGE` or `UNCERTAIN`, with support, counterexamples and confidence. Do not promote a candidate into generic Nigerian usage from Omah Lay alone.

For strong observations, record **observation → language mechanism → possible original-writing use**, plus evidence count, scope, uncertainty and originality boundary. Teach rhythmic, phonetic or character function, not reuse of a source expression. A future Nigerian Language Critic should be able to test natural Pidgin, speaker/context fit, dated language, purposeful switching, rhythm and character benefit, and excessive association with an existing song. These are future evaluation questions, not current automatic verdicts.

The optional entry format is `schemas/slang-entry-template.md`. `contemporary/slang/` is reserved for later *cross-artist*, dated language synthesis; do not populate it heavily from one artist. Source-song observations stay in song files until comparative evidence warrants promotion.

## Translation into original-writing tests

For a sufficiently supported observation, optionally record `GENERATION_RELEVANCE` as **observation → mechanism → possible original-writing use**, with source/song, timestamp or brief cue, evidence type, confidence, exceptions and a test against other songs. Keep the use abstract; do not reproduce identifiable lyric, tune, catchphrase or structure. A single song yields a candidate, not a Nigerian-music rule. Benchmark 001 is a generation experiment and does not by itself verify a songwriting hypothesis.

Record possible AI-writing failure signals as **testable hypotheses**, never blanket defects: mechanically equal syllable counts; excessively complete grammar; perfect-rhyme fixation or visual rhyme without aural rhyme; absent internal rhythm, compression, vocables, rests, shortened pronunciation, playfulness or melodic affordance; forced Pidgin, fake syntax, purposeless code-switching or stray slang; literary vocabulary mismatched to voice; weak vowel planning; information-heavy hooks; clever-looking but hard-to-sing lines; or every word carrying equal rhythmic weight. For each, cite audible or comparative evidence, note counterexamples, and test whether changing it improves a sung draft. Absence of any one device is not evidence of failure by itself.

## Project synthesis check

Compare these dimensions across independently completed song analyses. For each proposed project pattern, list supporting songs and source coverage, exceptions, confidence and whether later-project validation is needed. Do not convert unavailable portions into negative examples. Use the generative finding structure in `schemas/generative-finding-template.md` for candidate mechanisms; preserve song-specific techniques as such. Promote an experimental dimension only after cross-song testing shows useful discrimination and reproducibility.
